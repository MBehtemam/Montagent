# Question: the audio map's six frontier tickets

Sent blind and identical to three jurors dispatched in parallel (Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Fable 5.1), with no tools. One ballot per sub-question. Map: [#795](https://github.com/MBehtemam/Montagent/issues/795).

> **Status of each sub-question when this record was committed:** Q-A to Q-E were **decided by other sessions before this record landed** (ADR-0169, ADR-0170, ADR-0172, ADR-0173), so those ballots are kept for the record and are **not live**. Only **Q-F** ([#803](https://github.com/MBehtemam/Montagent/issues/803)) was still open. The ducking ticket ([#804](https://github.com/MBehtemam/Montagent/issues/804)) was not put to this court.

## Context given to every juror (facts, not opinions)

Montagent is a Rust video editor driven by AI agents. A project is a closed JSON document (unknown keys are schema errors) that agents edit by exact-string replace; rendering builds an ffmpeg filter graph. Elements are a flat list (ADR-0001); tracks are constrained lanes that own nothing (ADR-0004); times are absolute integer milliseconds (ADR-0005). ADR-0145: every capability keeps four invariants — literal values (no expressions, no references to other values), closed vocabulary, checkable by `validate` from the file and probed assets alone, editable by exact-string replace (each authored choice has its own string; derived things are changed via the literal that generates them). CapCut and Premiere are where precedent is looked for first; a capability with precedent in neither needs an owner-accepted rendered prototype.

Audio today: the only control is `volume` — a keyframeable linear multiplier (0 silent, 1 source, >1 amplify; dB was rejected in ADR-0055 because the identity value would be 0, which means "off" everywhere else in the format, and a reader can't read a level without log arithmetic), a flat top-level field on `audio` and `video` elements, peer of `speed`/`overrun`. Keyframes are `{t, v, ease}` records. Fades are volume keyframes; no mute field. The visual treatment chain is an ordered tagged `effects` list (blur, shadow, mask, colour filters); ADR-0040 says audio effects are "their own vocabulary on audio elements, not a branch of" it. The mix: per element atrim→atempo→aloop→volume→atrim,asetpts,adelay, then `amix normalize=0`, 48 kHz stereo, AAC 160k; keyframed volume applied as per-frame timed commands (ADR-0077). Visual `transition` elements (crossfade, wipe, slide, push; with an exact window between two elements) exist; the audio mix ignores them. `verify` measures the deliverable's loudness with an EBU R128 gate using the `ebur128` crate. ADR-0121: partial renders exist (a time range). Repo has no CI-run tests of audio content beyond render tests.

Planning map (destination: one ADR per audio capability with hand-off specs). Settled scope: in — loudness normalisation, pan/balance, channel ops, audio crossfades/transitions, EQ, compressor/limiter/gate, ffmpeg-native noise reduction + de-ess, reverb (no IR file), echo/delay, pitch shift, voice changers, pitch preservation under speed; ducking only as a write tool emitting volume keyframes. Effects attach to the element and to ONE small closed master/output stage; track-level effects are out of scope. Standing rule: every capability's ADR names a measured acceptance check (e.g. LUFS, true peak) beside the owner's ear; audio prototypes are rendered through the real pipeline as a loudness-matched A/B vs bypass.

Research findings ([`PRECEDENT.md`](../../audio-effects/PRECEDENT.md), [`FFMPEG-FILTERS.md`](../../audio-effects/FFMPEG-FILTERS.md)):
- Premiere: confirmed mechanism for 13/14 capabilities, each a named effect with typed, keyframable scalar params, attachable at clip/track/mix. Units: loudness -23 LUFS dialogue Auto-Match; export normalisation to R128/A85/BS.1770; pan -100..+100; EQ in Hz/dB/Q; Pitch Shifter semitones/cents/ratio; "Maintain Audio Pitch" checkbox on speed. Auto-ducking and Auto-Match write ordinary volume keyframes / clip gain. Audio crossfades (Constant Power, Constant Gain, Exponential Fade) are objects separate from video transitions. Premiere effects stack in order in a clip's effect rack. CapCut: no manual; marketing pages show loudness normalise (fixed -23 LUFS), Reduce noise on/off, Enhance voice 0–100, a pitch toggle on speed; the rest unconfirmed. Voice changers: precedent in neither as DSP (Premiere's are saved presets of e.g. Pitch Shifter; CapCut's opaque).
- ffmpeg 7.1 floor: every candidate filter is built in except `rubberband` (missing from CI's macOS Homebrew ffmpeg). Byte-identical on one build across reruns and thread counts; across builds/CPU flags `loudnorm` (dynamic), `superequalizer`, `firequalizer`, `afftdn`, asetrate pitch drift → hashes must pin to the CI floor build. Runtime commands (needed for keyframed params) accepted by biquad EQs, alimiter, acompressor, afftdn, anlmdn, firequalizer, atempo, rubberband, afir dry/wet; silently ignored by loudnorm, pan, aecho, deesser, superequalizer, acrossfade; agate threshold accepted but inert. Fixed latency: alimiter (attack; `latency=1` fixes), superequalizer 4095, afftdn 1200, anlmdn 384, firequalizer 480 samples — cancellable exactly with apad…atrim. loudnorm: dynamic mode outputs 192 kHz (needs aresample) and isn't byte-stable across builds; linear mode requires four measured_* values from a first pass. afade is sample-exact; acrossfade is replaceable by afade out/in on two chains summed in amix. aecho lengthens the stream; afir truncates tails. Today's keyframed volume can land up to one audio frame late (asendcmd on frame boundaries).

## Sub-questions

**Q-A (ticket "Which workflow does the audio map serve first?", [#798](https://github.com/MBehtemam/Montagent/issues/798)).** The owner wrote only "we need to support more audio effect like premier and after effect or capcut, currently audio feature that we support is less". The map runs on the working assumption "narrated video over a music bed" (normalisation, ducking, voice EQ + compression). What should the map serve first? Options: (a) confirm the narration-over-music assumption; (b) CapCut-style creative effects for short-form; (c) dialogue clean-up of recorded footage; (d) this cannot be decided without the owner — say what to ask.

**Q-B (ticket "Where does an audio effect sit on an element: an ordered list, flat fields, or both?", [#799](https://github.com/MBehtemam/Montagent/issues/799)).** Options: (a) an ordered list of tagged audio effects under its own key (not `effects`); (b) flat fields, peers of `volume`; (c) a mix — flat fields for order-free properties (e.g. pan) and an ordered list for the processing chain. Also say: the key name; where `volume` sits relative to the chain; whether the same kind may appear twice; how one entry is singled out for exact-string replace; what a `video` element's embedded audio carries.

**Q-C (ticket "Are new audio levels written in dB or as linear multipliers?", [#800](https://github.com/MBehtemam/Montagent/issues/800)).** Options: (a) dB for all new level parameters (EQ gain, thresholds, makeup, ceiling, loudness target), `volume` stays the only linear level; (b) linear everywhere for consistency with `volume`; (c) per-parameter documented choice. Also: how the unit shows in the key name (e.g. `gain_db`), and units for frequency, time, ratio, pan.

**Q-D (ticket "What is the master stage, where does it live, and what does it hold?", [#801](https://github.com/MBehtemam/Montagent/issues/801)).** Decide: where it sits in the document; its closed vocabulary; whether loudness normalisation is (i) a target the renderer reaches by a two-pass measurement, or (ii) a literal gain the agent writes after `verify` measures it, or (iii) something else; how `verify`'s R128 gate reads it; what a partial render or `preview` does with a whole-mix target.

**Q-E (ticket "What measured check must every audio capability's ADR name, and what runs it?", [#802](https://github.com/MBehtemam/Montagent/issues/802)).** Decide: whether the number is checked by `verify` on the deliverable, by a repo test fixture, or both; what tooling measures spectra/peaks; tolerance convention; how an audio prototype's loudness-matched A/B is produced and committed as evidence.

**Q-F (ticket "Does a transition carry the audio across its cut, or do audio crossfades get their own mechanism?", [#803](https://github.com/MBehtemam/Montagent/issues/803)).** Options: (a) every visual transition also crossfades the two elements' audio over its exact window; (b) audio crossfades are their own mechanism (separate from visual transitions, as in Premiere); (c) both — transitions carry audio by default with an opt-out, plus a separate audio-only crossfade for audio↔audio cuts. Also: the curve(s) and whether selectable; interaction with volume keyframes inside the window.
