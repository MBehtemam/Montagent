# Round 1 — ballots, verbatim

Question: [`round-1-question.md`](round-1-question.md). Three jurors, dispatched
in parallel, no juror seeing another's ballot.

---

🗳️ **Juror 1** (Claude Opus 5) — **Q1 VOTE: b**

**Reasoning:** The gap is genuinely one gap — "the file records values but not the relationship that produced them" — and the fix rule is genuinely one rule, because ADR-0015 already proved it works: a renderer-ignored claim about how the author computed a literal, re-derived and checked by `validate`. That much is shared. But the *content* of a derivation rule is not shared across axes, and (a) is where the design dies. A generic `derived` field whose rule "ranges over arithmetic" is an expression field with a friendly name. Even renderer-ignored, `validate` must evaluate it, and the file now contains a thing a reader cannot understand by reading — they must run the arithmetic in their head to know what the document asserts. That is the original disease with the blast radius reduced, not removed. It also silently reopens the closed-schema question: "R ranges over arithmetic" means the set of legal values is not named and published, it is generated. `fit` is the counter-model precisely because it is three words, not a formula: `cover` is a *named rule*, and its arithmetic lives in published rendering semantics, not in the document. Each axis gets the same treatment — a named field, a finite published vocabulary of rules, one legal value each — and the pattern is what binds them.

**Trade-offs:** (b) costs repetition: three or four field names, three or four enums, three or four validator re-derivations, and the standing risk that the enums accrete values one exemplar at a time. (a) would buy real economy — one field, one checker, any future axis covered free — and I am rejecting a genuinely cheaper design; the price is that it is the one design the standing principles forbid. (c) is the honest fallback if the axes turned out to share nothing, but they demonstrably do: all four are "a literal whose provenance is unrecorded," and letting four tickets each invent their own answer guarantees four incompatible shapes and no shared constraint to hold the fourth one to. (b) is also the only option that answers the first question before the fields, which the non-goals explicitly demand.

🗳️ **Juror 1** (Claude Opus 5) — **Q2 VOTE: Yes — a recorded, renderer-ignored id reference is a different object from the one rejected, and is permitted, but only where the declaration is provably unresolvable without it (today: the audio axis).**

**Reasoning:** ADR-0036's three grounds all attach to *liveness*, not to the id. "Breaks read-by-reading" — a live reference makes the rendered value depend on a second location; a recorded one leaves the literal authoritative and the reader still reads one number. "Degrades to a stale literal on the first shift" — that is a statement about a reference the renderer resolves at render time; a `validate`-only reference that goes stale *is precisely the finding*, it is the feature, not the failure mode. "A silent second author" — silence is the load-bearing word, and a renderer that never reads the field has no second author at all. So the rejection does not reach it. The audio measurement then converts "permitted" into "required on that axis": 11 of 22 text elements overlap two or more audio sources, so the partner is not inferable, and a `highlight` declaration that cannot name its partner asserts nothing. A field with no content is worse than no field. Rename fragility and dangling references are real, but they are `validate`'s ordinary work — a dangling id is a closed, decidable, document-local check, unlike the intent questions the format genuinely cannot answer. What I would hold firm on is that this does not become a general permission: an id reference is legal only when the axis has *measured* that inference fails, and the time-derivation axis has not (`end − 300` names its source by being on the same element).

**Trade-offs:** The format goes from zero element-to-element references to one, and zero is a much easier invariant to defend than "one, under conditions." Every rename tool, every splice, every future write verb now has an id-graph to keep consistent, and the cost lands on tools that today do not need to know elements can point at each other. Readers gain a second place to look — mitigated, but not erased, by the target being inert. And ADR-0012's win is partially given back, which should be said plainly rather than finessed.

🗳️ **Juror 1** (Claude Opus 5) — **Q3 VOTE: `error` with an advise-class repair where the declaration is directional; `review` with a refuse-class repair (`"none"`) where it is symmetric.**

**Reasoning:** The repair-class test is stated exactly: advise when the correct fix is fully determined by the document, the media and published semantics. A directional declaration *is* the author supplying the missing determinant. Having written "this timestamp is `end − 300`", the author has already answered the only question a reader would have to look at a frame to answer — which of the two numbers is stale. The derived one is, by declaration. So the fix is one legal value, exactly as `fit` yields one legal integer, and `fit` is an `error` with an advise repair for that reason. Downgrading to `review` here would mean refusing to state a value the document determines, which inverts the vocabulary: `review` is for "legal, renders, and you must look" — but a broken declaration is not a thing you look at a frame to adjudicate, it is an internal contradiction the document resolves itself. The symmetric case is genuinely the other object. "These two stay equal" names no source; when they differ, the document determines that something is wrong but not which side to move, and that is the textbook refuse-class condition. The honest question is then whether symmetric declarations should exist at all — a half-binding that can only ever produce a `"none"` is weak, and ADR-0036 already flagged exactly this weakness. My position: allow them only where the axis cannot express direction, and treat a proposed symmetric rule as evidence the vocabulary for that axis is underspecified.

**Trade-offs:** `error` means a violated declaration refuses the render, so a mistyped or stale declaration can block work on a file that would render fine — the declaration acquires teeth it did not have when it was a comment in the author's head, and authors may respond by not declaring. The advise repair also carries a subtler risk: it will confidently state "rewrite the derived value" in cases where the author actually moved the derived value on purpose and forgot to drop the declaration, so the advised repair undoes an intentional edit. The escape value (`fit`'s `literal`) is what makes this survivable and must exist on every axis. Splitting severity by directionality also means one field can emit two different classes, which is a small complication to `validate`'s contract that has to be documented, not discovered.

🗳️ **Juror 1** (Claude Opus 5) — **Q4 VOTE: Ship the pattern plus exactly one concrete field — the time-derivation field (`end − 300`, 4 of 52) — and graduate audio, timestamp-coincidence and motion as separate tickets against it.**

**Reasoning:** A pattern with no instance is untested architecture; the ADR would be asserting that renderer-ignored declaration generalizes beyond rasters without ever having moved it off rasters once. One instantiation proves the transplant and gives every later ticket a worked example to argue against. But the time-derivation axis is the only one that can ship *in the same decision*, because it is the only one that needs nothing the ADR has not already settled: the source is on the same element, no id reference, a direction, a finite rule vocabulary, an advise-class repair — it exercises the pattern end to end on the cheapest possible terrain. Audio is well measured (11 of 22 is the strongest number here) but it is the axis that spends the Q2 permission, and reintroducing element-to-element references after ADR-0012 deserves its own record with its own dissent, not a rider on a pattern ADR. Timestamp-coincidence overlaps `shift`'s existing preamble and needs that boundary worked out first — otherwise two mechanisms report the same fact. Motion has no census, no measurement and no proposed shape; on this project's own evidence bar it does not graduate, and including it would be the "four bespoke fields" the non-goals forbid.

**Trade-offs:** Shipping one field risks the pattern being overfit to it — the time axis is the easy case, and a pattern validated only there may not survive contact with audio's id reference or motion's two-element coupling, which means the later tickets may have to amend the pattern rather than merely instantiate it. It also leaves the best-measured axis (audio, 11 of 22) unshipped while the weaker-measured one (4 of 52) lands, which is awkward to justify on evidence alone and rests instead on architectural cost — that reasoning must be stated explicitly in the ADR or it will read as inconsistent. And a pattern with one instance and three open tickets invites drift: each later ticket will be tempted to bend the pattern to its axis, so the ADR should state which parts are fixed (renderer-ignored, `validate`-only consumer, closed enum, escape value, repair-class rule) and which each axis chooses (field name, rule vocabulary, whether it names an id).

🗳️ **Juror 2** (Claude Sonnet 5) — **Q1 VOTE: b**

**Reasoning:** The evidence bar itself is uneven across axes (4-of-52, 11-of-22, a preamble, and nothing at all for motion), which is disqualifying for (a): a generic `derived`-with-arithmetic-rule field reintroduces expressions by the back door — R "ranges over arithmetic" is exactly the evaluation the format rejected, and it would force `validate` to interpret an open-ended rule language rather than match against a closed vocabulary. That collapses the `fit` precedent's core property: `fit`'s power is that `cover`/`contain`/`literal` is a finite enum validate can exhaustively re-derive and check, never a computation it executes. (c) ignores that all four exemplars share one shape: a renderer-ignored, validate-consumed declaration recording author intent about a relationship already implicit in the data — that shared shape is real and deciding it once avoids relitigating "is this a live reference," "does it carry a repair," "is it directional" four separate times. (b) preserves the shared consumption contract (renderer-ignored, validate-only, closed rule vocabulary, advise/refuse repair discipline) while letting each axis's field carry only the specific rule vocabulary its domain needs — `endMinusOffset` for time, `syncPartner` for audio — without pretending one arithmetic-generic field can express both without evaluating expressions.

**Trade-offs:** (a) is the most reusable on paper and avoids naming four fields, but it either becomes a real expression language (violating inert-data) or a thin wrapper that still needs per-domain enums underneath, at which point it's (b) with extra indirection. (c) is honest about the uneven evidence but forfeits the chance to fix the repair-class and severity questions once; four independent ADRs risk four subtly different answers to Q2/Q3, which is worse for a reader than four fields sharing one contract.

🗳️ **Juror 2** (Claude Sonnet 5) — **Q2 VOTE: It is a different object, but only if the reference is inert data validate resolves, never a pointer the renderer or any live process dereferences — and even then it must earn its place per axis.**

**Reasoning:** ADR-0036's rejection turns on *who reads it*: a reference the renderer dereferences at render time creates a second author for rendered geometry and goes stale silently on shift. A reference that only `validate` reads, at validate time, on demand, produces no silent staleness — because staleness is exactly what validate is checking for and reporting. The object is structurally closer to a foreign key checked by a constraint validator than to a live pointer resolved by a renderer. ADR-0012's "zero element-to-element references" was about removing a *renderer-consumed* reference (`box:"<id>"` presumably affected layout); it doesn't obviously bind a validate-only id-reference, and the audio exemplar's own measurement (11 of 22 ambiguous by timeline overlap) proves there is no substitute — inference cannot fill this gap, so if the audio axis instantiates at all, naming the partner id is not optional, it is the declaration.

**Trade-offs:** This does reopen the door ADR-0012 closed, in spirit if not in mechanism: rename fragility and dangling references are real costs, and "the renderer ignores it" is a distinction a future reader has to already understand to trust the reference is inert — that's a second place to look even if it's not a second place the renderer looks. The mitigation is that `validate` already must resolve it and can flag a dangling id as an `error` with a `"none"` repair (the fix requires knowing the intended new partner), so the cost is bounded and self-diagnosing rather than silent.

🗳️ **Juror 2** (Claude Sonnet 5) — **Q3 VOTE: Directional declarations are `error` with advise-class repair; symmetric declarations are `review` with refuse-class repair — the class must track whether the document itself names a source.**

**Reasoning:** The `fit` precedent already establishes the exact discriminator: `error`+advise applies when "the rule determines exactly one legal integer" — i.e., when the document, on its own terms, says which value is authoritative. A directional derivation (`end − 300`, `highlight` window keyed to a named audio source) names its source explicitly; when the derived value diverges, rewriting the derived value from the source is not an inference about intent, it's arithmetic the declaration already licensed — that's `error`/advise by the same logic as `fit`. A symmetric declaration ("these two stay equal", "land exactly on the shared instant") carries no source/derived asymmetry — when it breaks, both numbers are equally suspect, and picking one to rewrite *is* asserting intent no document carries, which is precisely the `review`+`"none"` case per the classing rubric ("legal, renders, and you must look at a frame to know if it was meant").

**Trade-offs:** This means the same underlying gap (a broken relationship) produces different severities depending on field shape, which is a subtler rule for authors and tool-builders to hold in their heads than "any broken declaration is always X." But collapsing to one answer either wrongly promotes symmetric breaks to auto-fixable (violating the refuse-class guarantee, which no flag or write tool may lift) or wrongly downgrades directional breaks to review when the fix was in fact fully determined — both are worse than the added rule complexity.

🗳️ **Juror 2** (Claude Sonnet 5) — **Q4 VOTE: Ship the pattern plus the two axes that clear a measurement bar now (time-derivation, audio-link); graduate timestamp-coincidence and motion as separate tickets pending their own measurement.**

**Reasoning:** ADR-0063 already set the precedent for how much evidence this class of decision demands before committing a concrete mechanism — it declined to add offset-drift detection to `compare` specifically because the candidate population was unmeasured and would "explode." Time-derivation (4/52, a concrete rule, `end − 300`) and audio-link (11/22, provably unresolvable by inference) both clear that bar: they have real fixture evidence and, per Q2, the audio one has no substitute for a named reference at all. Timestamp-coincidence has only `shift`'s partial preamble, not a fixture measurement, and motion has zero — no census, no proposed shape, nothing to instantiate. Shipping a pattern with zero fields is a paper decision no one can hold up against `fit`'s "does it actually work" test; shipping four fields without measuring two of them repeats exactly the mistake ADR-0063 avoided.

**Trade-offs:** Splitting the ADR into pattern+2 now and 2 tickets later risks the graduated axes drifting from the shared contract if the follow-up tickets are decided by different people at different times without re-reading this ADR — the same fragmentation risk (c) posed at Q1, just deferred rather than avoided. The alternative (pattern alone, all four as tickets) is safer against scope creep but ships a decision with no concrete proof it composes with `validate`'s repair/severity machinery, which is the whole point of grounding this in the `fit` precedent.

🗳️ **Juror 3** (Claude Fable 5.1) — **Q1 VOTE: (b) — one pattern, several named fields, each with a closed rule vocabulary**

**Reasoning:** The four exemplars share one diagnosis (ADR-0037: provenance, not computation) and one already-proven shape (`fit`: renderer-ignored, `validate`-consumed, closed enum, `literal`-style escape value). That shared shape *is* the decision; it must be written down once so the fourth field cannot drift into something the renderer reads or an expression. But the *rules* differ irreducibly per axis: `end − 300` is a timestamp-with-offset, a same-endpoint/same-rate coupling is a motion relation, a highlight-to-audio link is a partner assertion with no arithmetic at all. A closed enum per axis keeps each rule set small enough that `validate` can re-derive it exactly, the way `cover`/`contain` determine one integer. The pattern-level ADR should fix the invariants: no renderer reads the field; the value is a member of a published enum, never an expression; `validate` re-derives and compares; a `literal`/`none` value means no claim.

**Trade-offs:** (a) fails the inert-data principle the moment R "ranges over arithmetic": `{"derived": {"from": "end", "rule": "minus", "arg": 300}}` is a tiny expression language, exactly what the Out-of-scope ruling forbids, and its rule set is open by construction. (c) produces four fields with four shapes and no guarantee the second one doesn't quietly become a live reference; it also wastes the `fit` precedent, which already answers the structural question. (b) costs some duplication in schema docs and a small enum per axis, which is the same price `fit` already paid.

🗳️ **Juror 3** (Claude Fable 5.1) — **Q2 VOTE: Yes — a renderer-ignored id reference resolved only by `validate` is a different object; allow it, with a dangling id as a `review`, not a hard error, and only on axes where the partner is unrecoverable from data**

**Reasoning:** ADR-0036's three grounds were all about the *renderer* consuming the reference: it breaks read-by-reading because the rendered value isn't in the file; it degrades to a stale literal on shift; it gives geometry a second author. A declaration that the renderer ignores triggers none of these: every rendered value is still the literal on the element, `shift` still rewrites literals and the declaration either still holds or `validate` reports it, and no element's geometry has a second author because the field writes nothing. What survives is documentary: "this window was calibrated against `audio-3`." That is a claim about the author, the same category as `fit`. The audio measurement settles it — 11 of 22 ambiguous means there is no partner-free formulation of the declaration; refusing the id means refusing the axis.

**Trade-offs:** Rename fragility and dangling references do come back, in a milder form: a rename leaves a claim pointing nowhere. That should be a `review` finding ("declared partner `audio-3` does not exist"), not an `error`, because the file still renders correctly; the reader learns the claim is unverifiable, which is strictly more than they knew before. The "second place to look" cost is real but bounded: the second place is *within the same element*, next to the literal it annotates, and it never changes what is on screen. The discipline to hold: id references appear only in declaration fields, never in anything a renderer or `shift` consumes, so ADR-0012's "zero references the renderer follows" remains true.

🗳️ **Juror 3** (Claude Fable 5.1) — **Q3 VOTE: Directional declarations → `error` with advise-class repair; symmetric or partner-only declarations → `review` with `"none"`**

**Reasoning:** The repair rule already decides this. Advise-class applies when the fix is fully determined by the document plus published semantics. A directional declaration (`start` is `end − 300`, or "this keyframe is anchored to the cut at `id`") states the source and the derived side; when it breaks, the only self-consistent document is the one where the derived literal is rewritten, so `validate` can state the integer, exactly as `fit` does. That the author *might* have meant to change the source instead is not a document-level ambiguity; it's the author changing the declaration, which is a separate edit. Symmetric declarations ("these two stay equal") name no direction, so which literal is stale is not in the document; that is the textbook refuse-class case and must be `review`, because the file renders legally and someone has to look at a frame. The audio partner link is a third case: it can't be arithmetically violated at all (there's no rule to re-derive), so its only findings are "partner missing" (`review`) or nothing.

**Trade-offs:** Making directional violations `error` means a document that renders fine is refused-class in `validate` output, which will feel heavy when the author simply forgot to update the declaration. The alternative — `review` everywhere — throws away the directional information the author paid to record and forces a frame look for something arithmetic already answers. Since the repair is advise-class, the cost of the `error` is one literal rewrite, which is cheap; the cost of `review` is a manual inspection, which is not. Symmetric declarations should therefore be discouraged in the enum design: if an axis can express its rule directionally, it should.

🗳️ **Juror 3** (Claude Fable 5.1) — **Q4 VOTE: Ship the pattern plus one field — the time-derivation axis (4-of-52, directional, arithmetic-checkable) — and graduate audio, coincidence, and motion as separate tickets against the pattern**

**Reasoning:** A pattern ADR with zero instances is untestable: nothing exercises the invariants, and the first real field would relitigate them anyway. The time-derivation axis is the cleanest first instance: it is measured, its rule set is trivially closed (a small offset enum or a fixed set like `end-minus`, matching the fixture's `end − 300`), it is directional so Q3 gives it advise-class repairs, and it needs *no* id reference, so it proves the pattern without simultaneously deciding Q2 in code. The audio axis is well measured but is the one that forces the id-reference question and has no arithmetic to check; it deserves its own ticket that cites Q2's ruling and designs the dangling-id finding. Timestamp-coincidence overlaps `shift`'s preamble and needs a scoping pass to avoid recreating ADR-0063's offset-explosion problem. Motion has no census and no shape; graduating it now would be exactly the "bespoke field without evidence" the non-goals forbid.

**Trade-offs:** Shipping only one field means the audio gap — arguably the most painful, since the partner is provably unrecoverable — waits one more round. Shipping two (time plus audio) would be defensible but couples the pattern decision to the id-reference decision, so a reversal on Q2 would drag the pattern down with it. Shipping the pattern alone is cheapest but produces an ADR nobody can validate against a fixture, which this repo's own practice (census before shape) argues against.
