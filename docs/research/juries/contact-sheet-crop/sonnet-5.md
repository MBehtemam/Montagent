🗳️ **Juror 3** (Claude Sonnet 5) — **VOTE: C, refuse `--crop` composed with a range**

**Reasoning:** The founding experiment is the entire warrant for this effort, and it names the exact failure mode this crop reproduces: a rule that structurally cannot see a defect, whose silence reads as coverage. The photo-07 test isn't a footnote — it's the load-bearing proof that a plausible, well-intentioned selection rule can be blind and still look thorough. A caller-typed crop is worse than the bounding-box sampler in one respect: the bounding-box agent's blindness was at least uniform and disclosable as a rule ("this method cannot see color"). A caller-typed crop's blindness is call-specific and content-dependent — it excludes whatever the caller didn't think to include, which is exactly the shape of blind spot no fixed `blind_to` line can characterize, because it changes every invocation. Fact 4 confirms this doesn't even fit the taxonomy that disclosure depends on. And fact 3 proves the disclosure escape hatch doesn't reliably work: an accurate disclosure was read past because the tool's own documentation promised something else — so even a perfect prose warning is not guaranteed to overcome a caller's prior belief that "I cropped, therefore I looked." A caller who typed the crop already believes they know where the defect would be, which is precisely the belief the whole effort exists to distrust. Meanwhile the two-step workflow (whole-frame sheet, then lossless single-instant `--crop`) already exists, costs nothing new, and puts the crop only where it belongs: after a human or agent has looked at the whole document and chosen to zoom in with full awareness of what they're leaving out, one instant at a time, not baked into 18 tiles' worth of an "I checked everything" artifact.

**Trade-offs:** This costs the effort its highest-measured-value mode — real, 2.33x legibility gain, and the only way word-08 stays legible past ~30 tiles. Option A trades detection power for a disclosure that the evidence base (fact 3) shows can fail exactly when it matters most, and does so silently to the reader, not to the system — the tool did its job and the report was still wrong. Option B looked attractive but fact 5 kills it on inspection: there is no region vocabulary to derive from safely (text ink boxes refuse under rotation/scale/RTL), so "derived, not caller-typed" is a promise the document model can't currently keep, and shipping a refusal-prone derivation is its own honesty problem in disguise. Option C's cost is real: fine-detail defects lose their best tool, and the two-step workaround requires the agent to already suspect a location, which is not guaranteed. That's an acceptable cost because it's recoverable by disclosure done right (the sheet can and should say "want to look closer at a suspect instant? use `frame --crop --at`"), whereas photo-07's failure under a shipped crop is not recoverable — it's just gone, unrecorded, in a mode that advertises completeness.
