🗳️ **Juror** (Opus) — **VOTE: Q1: Exact numeric coincidence only. Q2: Reuse the existing grouped-deltas-as-facts convention. Q3: No severity — stay purely descriptive. Q4: Confirm — explicitly out of scope; the prior ADRs did not miss a closable path.**

**Reasoning:**

**Q1.** Exact coincidence is a *fact readable from one file* — two numbers are equal, or they are not. A fixed-offset relationship is a *hypothesis inferred across versions*: "B − A was 300 and still is 300" requires deciding that 300 was meaningful rather than incidental. With 120 boundary endpoints and 60 elements, the set of pairwise offsets that "held" between any two versions is quadratic and almost entirely noise; every pair of instants that both stayed put trivially preserves its offset, so the check either reports nothing (if you require the offset to break) or reports thousands of coincidental arithmetic facts. Worse, the offset framing has no way to distinguish a preserved relationship from two unrelated records that both happened not to move — which is exactly the failure mode ADR-0039 already paid for once, where an inferred relationship (`group` as a motion unit) collided with real data. Exact equality is the only relationship the file itself asserts without the tool guessing. ADR-0036's own wording — "two instants that were bit-identical" — is the scope; broadening it re-imports inference by the back door.

Note the fixture bears this out asymmetrically: 92/120 endpoints coincide, so destroyed-coincidence has real, dense material to work with; zero cross-element same-property keyframe matches exist, so coupled-motion-drift is a check that will correctly report nothing on today's real data. That asymmetry is fine and expected — it is the difference between a check that fires on noise and one that stays silent until a real relationship exists. Adding fixed offsets would invert it, making the currently-silent check the loud one, on the weakest evidence.

**Q2.** The existing convention already does the required work: it states a fact, groups the majority behavior, and names the exception ("19 of 20 … moved +800 ms; vo-quiz-answer did not"). A destroyed coincidence *is* that sentence — "vo-quiz-answer's out and bg-photo-3's in were both 30603; bg-photo-3's in is now 31403" is the same grammar with a group of size two. Inventing a second report shape means the caller must learn two vocabularies for one tool and gives every future `compare` check licence to invent a third. ADR-0032's slack drift already fit in this shape; there is no property of these two hazards that the shape cannot carry. One tool, one report grammar, is also what keeps the file-and-output "understandable by reading it" principle honest.

**Q3.** No severity. Severity in Montaget is load-bearing precisely because `render` refuses on `error` — severity is not a mood, it is an enforcement contract, and `compare` has no enforcement relationship with `render`. Beyond the plumbing, the fact/judgment boundary settles it on the merits: a destroyed coincidence is frequently *the entire point of the edit* — the author deliberately broke a cut alignment. `compare` cannot know which, and assigning `review` to something the author just did on purpose is judgment about intent, which ADR-0006/0061 forbid. Borrowing `validate`'s vocabulary without `validate`'s boundary discipline or `render`'s enforcement would make the words mean two different things in two tools — the worst outcome for a design whose whole claim is legibility. Matching slack (no severity) keeps `compare` a uniformly factual instrument.

**Q4.** Confirm — out of scope, and the prior ADRs did not miss anything. The reasoning is structural, not a gap in effort: `compare` is a differ; a coupling that never held has no prior state in which it held, so there is nothing for any diff to find. `validate` is stateless and cannot distinguish a desynced lower-third from a deliberate staggered entrance — ADR-0039 already paid full price for trying, false-positiving on 5/5 real groups. The only closure is a schema field declaring intended coupling, which converts the inference into an author-supplied fact — and that is a separate, unproposed design with its own costs (a new declarative construct every author must maintain, and a new class of stale declaration). It should not be smuggled into a mechanics ticket. Out of scope is not "unsolvable forever"; it is "requires a different decision, made deliberately."

**Trade-offs:**

- **Q1** gives up the genuine case where an author held a deliberate 300 ms lead-in and lost it — `compare` will stay silent. Accepted: that case is indistinguishable from arithmetic noise without a declaration of intent, and is a subset of the Q4 problem. Note also a cost in scope: exact-coincidence destruction on this fixture would key off 92 coincident endpoints, so the design must still report only coincidences *destroyed between the two versions*, never the census ADR-0036 rejected.
- **Q2** costs some expressiveness — a destroyed coincidence's "before" state (they were equal) has to be stated explicitly rather than implied by a bespoke shape. Cheap, and worth it against a second grammar.
- **Q3** costs the caller a ranking signal: all `compare` facts arrive flat, and a reader of a large diff must judge which matter. That judgment is correctly theirs; the alternative is `compare` guessing intent, which is the exact failure ADR-0039 documented, and severity words that don't gate `render` would be severity in name only.
- **Q4** leaves a real hazard open — a lower-third born desynced ships desynced. The alternative (a coupling field) is a larger design that should be proposed on its own evidence, not bolted onto mechanics for two hazards that are, by construction, about relationships that once held.
