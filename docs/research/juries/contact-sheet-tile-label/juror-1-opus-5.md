🗳️ **Juror 1** (Claude Opus 5 (1M context)) — **VOTE:**
Q1: (a) gutter strip beneath each tile, 11% of tile height — no overlay.
Q2: (c) both, with the **text sigil load-bearing** and the visual treatment redundant; document-derived tiles are the **unmarked default**, non-derived classes always carry the mark, and every tile's class is also stated in words on the range-level provenance list.
Q3: Repeat the full presence set on every line. No delta. (If size ever bites, hoist the document-wide always-on set to a declared header — never accumulate state across lines.)
Q4: Fixed arity — same fields, same order, every tile. Character count may vary; the *field list* may not. Degradation is sheet-wide, never per-tile.
Q5: Yes — a floor of **8 px served type**. When content doesn't fit at the floor: drop the optional identifying field for the whole sheet (uniformly), and if the mandatory fields still don't fit, **refuse**, as the 140 px cliff already refuses. Never shrink below the floor.
Q6: No. The boundary may not be dropped. It may be **losslessly re-encoded** as a signed delta from the instant (`1240+16`, always printed, `+0` when equal), which costs ~3 chars instead of 7 and keeps the number recoverable.
Q7: (c) the tool vendors its own UI face for its own chrome — an OFL/Apache face with a full tabular-figure digit set, hard-failing at build time if absent. Never the project's font, never a system font.
Q8: The label names the element(s) whose presence **changed at this run's own boundary**, signed: `+id` entered, `-id` departed. Tie-break: highest layer order. Departure-only runs name the departed element with its minus sign. The sheet's first tile has no boundary delta, so it names the highest-layer element that is not always-on for the *whole document*; if none exists, the field prints an explicit placeholder and stays present.

**Reasoning:**

**Q1.** The defects this feature exists to catch live in pixels, and one of them is *absence* of pixels (text recoloured into its card). An overlay spends the one resource that cannot be reconstructed — image area — to save area that the accepted geometry has already budgeted. Worse, an overlay's own legibility depends on what is under it, so the label's readability would vary with the content it is describing: exactly backwards. The 11% strip is measured, is already inside the 180/140 cliffs, and is free of that coupling. Burning in also makes the tile no longer a faithful crop of what `frame --at` returns, which breaks the reproduce-the-tile property the sampled-instant decision was bought with.

**Q2.** I partly reject the framing: the label strip is not the only text channel. The provenance list has effectively unbounded width and is already mandated per tile, so tile class belongs there in unabbreviated words — that is the record. But the list is not what an agent looks at while scanning the grid, so the tile itself needs a mark too. Between the two tile-side options, visual treatment alone is not acceptable as the load-bearing carrier: a tint or a dashed rule is a perceptual claim of exactly the kind the record already flags as non-re-derivable, it can be swamped by tile content, and it survives no quotation, crop, or grayscale reproduction. So a one-character sigil is load-bearing and the visual treatment is redundancy for the fast read. On marked-vs-absence: mark the exceptional classes, define document-derived by absence. The dangerous confusion is one-directional — a synthetic sample passing as document-derived — and the mark is generated *for* synthetics, so absence cannot be forged by the tool. This also means the common case pays zero label characters, which matters given 295/chars. The one residual risk (a reader who does not know registers exist, seeing an all-unmarked sheet, and not knowing whether marks are even in play) is closed by the provenance list naming a class on every line, including `document` on the plain ones.

**Q3.** A delta is the trial failure in miniature. Under a delta, a line that says nothing is indistinguishable from a line whose author had nothing to say — silence reading as coverage — and it forces the reader to reconstruct state by accumulation, which is precisely the error the third agent made when it reconstructed coverage instead of observing it. It also destroys the ability to quote one line as evidence. The cost argument does not carry here: the 28,410-character figure was resolved *stacks* (geometry), not ids; 18 lines of ~122 characters is ~2.2 KB, an order of magnitude below the sheet answer's own budget headroom and nowhere near the 20x multiplier the split was designed to avoid. The 8 always-on chrome ids are the only real redundancy, and if that ever needs removing, remove it by declaring the always-on set once as a fact about the whole document — a statement, not a running difference.

**Q4.** Varying the field list makes a missing field ambiguous between "this tile has no such element" and "this tile's label ran out of room." That ambiguity is the house pattern of this entire failure mode, and it is worse than either honest alternative. Fixed arity also kills the 1.46x within-sheet type spread at its root for the mandatory fields, so the reader calibrates once. The important corollary is that elision must therefore be a property of the *sheet*, not of a tile: if the busiest tile cannot carry the identifying field at the floor, no tile carries it, and the type size stays near-uniform. A sheet that is uniformly less informative is honest; a sheet whose quietest tiles are conveniently detailed and whose busiest tile — statistically the likeliest to hide a defect — is the one that got silently thinned is not.

**Q5.** Yes, and I reject using 6.3 px as the number. That figure came from one primed observer who knew what the defects were; it is a ceiling on what was *ever* read, not a floor on what can be read cold, and the accepted record already flags it as non-re-derivable. Treating an optimistic single-observer datum as a design minimum is how a spec inherits a perceptual claim it cannot defend. 8 px carries visible margin over it and is reachable: the 25-char reference sits at 11.5 px @180 and 8.9 px @140, so the identifying field survives the floor in the normal case, and the floor bites only on candidates (like a full presence set) that were already rejected. On non-fit: uniform sheet-wide field drop first, then refuse. Refusal is already this feature's accepted answer to "cannot serve this honestly," and a label nobody can read is a silence wearing the appearance of a label — the same defect as a sampler that reports coverage it never had.

**Q6.** No, and the arithmetic argues against elision rather than for it. Because the two numbers differ by less than one frame period, a reader who sees only the instant will reasonably take it *for* the boundary and be wrong by up to 39 ms — a small, plausible, silent error, which is the worst kind. And because the difference is bounded below 1000/fps, the field compresses losslessly: print it as a signed delta. The upstream decision requires the label to print both numbers; a recoverable encoding prints both. Truncation does not. I would also require the `+0` case to print, so that "on-grid boundary" is an assertion rather than an absence.

**Q7.** The project's own font is disqualified three times over: it may be Thai-only, it may have no digits at all while the label is mostly digits, and the main fixture already declares a path that does not exist. A chrome element that fails when the *document* is unusual is a chrome element that fails hardest on the projects most likely to be broken. System fonts are disqualified by six platform targets: the label is load-bearing for detection, so its metrics and therefore the fitted type size must be identical everywhere, or the 8 px floor means a different thing per platform and the measurements stop being re-derivable. Vendoring costs a licence obligation and some binary size, and pays for a label that renders identically on all six targets regardless of what the document declares. Tabular figures specifically, since the payload is columns of milliseconds. And the build should fail loudly if the face is missing rather than falling back — the prototype quietly drawing with a font that happened to sit in the fixture directory is the exact accident this closes.

**Q8.** The question's own objection is right: "not present in every tile of this sheet" is range-dependent and therefore disqualifying — the same instant would label differently depending on what was asked for, which breaks reproducibility for no gain. The fix is to stop asking what is *discriminating* and ask what is *causal*: each tile exists because the filtered presence set changed at its boundary, so the label names that change. This is computed from the cut list at one boundary, so it is identical no matter what range contains it; it is mechanical; it involves no judgement about what matters, so it clears the no-verdicts line; and it excludes always-on chrome automatically, because chrome never changes — no special-casing needed. Layer order is the tie-break because it is total and a tie is already an error, so the rule is defined everywhere. The signed prefix is not cosmetic: a departure-only run means the named element is *not on screen* in this tile, and naming it unsigned would license pinning that tile's bad pixels on something absent — the per-tile misattribution hazard that motivated splitting the provenance list in the first place. The sheet's first tile has no causal boundary (nothing changed; it is the opening state), so it falls back to a state-based rule, and "always-on" there must be evaluated over the whole document, never the range, for the same range-independence reason. If that fallback finds nothing, the field prints a placeholder rather than vanishing, per Q4.

**Trade-offs:**

- **Q1** keeps 11% of every tile's height spent on chrome, which is real pixels lost at the 92–140 px range where fine detail already dies. Accepted: the measurements were taken with the strip in place and the cliffs are calibrated to it.
- **Q2** costs a character on marked tiles and asks the reader to know that unmarked means document-derived. The alternative reading — mark everything — costs characters on all 18 tiles to defend against a confusion that only runs one way. Pure visual (b) was rejected because it makes the register claim perceptual and unquotable; pure text with no tint (a alone) was rejected because the tint is nearly free and the scan is visual.
- **Q3** ships ~2 KB of repeated ids, and the 8 chrome ids per line are pure redundancy. That is the price of per-line self-containment, and it is small against the 1,413-character answer the feature is protecting.
- **Q4** means the whole sheet gets less informative when one tile is crowded — the busiest tile taxes the quietest ones. The alternative taxes precisely the tile most likely to hide something, which is worse.
- **Q5**'s 8 px is more conservative than the measured 6.3 px, so it will refuse or drop a field in some cases that a primed observer could in fact have read. That is deliberate: I would rather lose a field than inherit a one-observer perceptual claim as a hard spec constant. The number itself is the weakest thing in my ballot — it is margin-over-a-single-datum, not a measurement, and it deserves a cold-observer check before it is treated as settled.
- **Q6**'s delta encoding is slightly less immediately readable than two full numbers, and a reader must do one subtraction. It is lossless, which truncation is not.
- **Q7** takes on a font licence, binary weight, and a hard build dependency, and it means the label's typeface never matches the project's visual language. All three are cheap next to a label that cannot render digits because the document declared a display face.
- **Q8** yields labels that sometimes name an element that is not visible in the tile (departures). The minus sign is doing heavy work there, and if it is ever dropped or lost in reproduction the rule becomes actively misleading — which is another reason the sign belongs in the provenance list's prose as well as in the label.
