# Juror 1 — Opus 5

Verbatim ballot. Given [`BRIEF.md`](BRIEF.md) cold, blind to the other jurors and to the
author's recommendations.

---

🗳️ **Juror 1** (Opus 5) — **VOTE Q1: (a) — add explicit geometry in v1, as a fixed-arity, fully-spelled parameter set whose omission means exactly the ADR-0068 inscribed default.**

**Reasoning:** The decisive fact on the record is that an off-centre circle, a rounded rect, and a non-inscribed ellipse are *inexpressible by any means* — there is no composition workaround, unlike vignette and glow, which ADR-0040 could defer precisely because it named an alternative expression. A closed, published vocabulary (ADR-0017, ADR-0040) means "not yet specified" is not a soft gap an author can route around; it is a hard wall. Montaget's own stated shaping principle is that primitives are shaped by what a video editor must express, never by what one project happens to use, and the fixture is evidence a capability is needed, never evidence one is unneeded — so "zero evidence for explicit geometry" is exactly the argument the project has already ruled inadmissible. The second half of the record clinches it: the param-less form is *derived*, so a 68x68 → 68x120 resize silently changes the mask with no edit to the mask member. That is a latent action-at-a-distance in a file whose entire premise is that reading it tells you what it means. Explicit geometry is the only way an author can say "I meant this circle, not whatever my rect implies." On ADR-0049's transfer: the four-clause rule is about admitting surface into a closed vocabulary, and parameters are surface; I hold it transfers, and explicit mask geometry clears it — fixed arity per shape, bounded/integer pixel parameters in the element's own units, a documented identity (omission = the inscribed default), and not composable from existing members (nothing else cuts a shape).

**Trade-offs:** Cost is real and should be admitted: v1 ships parameters with no fixture exercising them, which risks getting a detail wrong under load (ADR-0075's lesson about asserting untested facts). It also widens `mask` from one uncontroversial member into the largest effect in the vocabulary. Option (b) is the cheaper, more disciplined-looking vote and I reject it because deferral here is not neutral — it leaves a whole class of authoring gesture unsayable behind a closed schema, and it leaves the derived-default footgun with no escape. Mitigation for (a)'s risk: keep arity minimal (centre + radius for `circle`; centre + two radii for `ellipse`; corner radius for `rect`), all integers in unscaled element units per ADR-0012's integer/float split, and spell an example literal, which is precisely what ADR-0040 failed to do and what ADR-0049 did right.

🗳️ **Juror 1** (Opus 5) — **VOTE Q2: Element-local, with `(0,0)` at the element rect's top-left regardless of the `origin` keyword.**

**Reasoning:** Frame-absolute is wrong for a reason stronger than taste: `clip` already occupies the frame-absolute slot, and the glossary goes out of its way twice to say mask and clip are different things ("Shaped and soft masks are a different thing and are not this"; "_Avoid_: clip"). Giving mask the same coordinate space as `clip` would make two distinct concepts numerically interchangeable in the common case and invite exactly the confusion the glossary is trying to prevent. Element-local also means a masked element stays correct when it is moved: changing `x`/`y` should never require rewriting the mask, and under frame-absolute it always would. On the origin sub-question: `origin` is a *placement* keyword — it says which point of the box lands on `(x, y)` — and it is not a re-parameterisation of the box's interior. The rect is `(0, 0, width, height)` in unscaled units under every origin; only where that rect sits on the frame changes. Letting `origin` also shift the mask's coordinate origin would mean flipping `origin` from `top-left` to `center` silently relocates the mask by half the box — a second instance of the same action-at-a-distance I objected to in Q1. This happens to match the shipped renderer, but I am voting it on the model's internal logic, not on deference to unratified code (ADR-0075's caution is well taken and I treat the code here as corroboration, not authority).

**Trade-offs:** The cost is that an author who thinks in `center` origins must still write mask coordinates from the top-left, which reads as a small inconsistency inside one element. I accept that because the alternative makes mask numbers depend on a field that has nothing to do with the mask. A `center`-relative mask space is the one genuinely defensible rival — it makes the common "circle slightly off-centre" case read as a small signed offset — but it forfeits the "the rect is always `(0,0,w,h)`" invariant that ADR-0012's flat model rests on, and it makes `origin` load-bearing for effect semantics.

🗳️ **Juror 1** (Opus 5) — **VOTE Q3: Pre-transform element space — the mask rides `scale` and `rotation`. The indirect animation is acceptable and should be stated explicitly, not treated as a violation.**

**Reasoning:** This follows from Q2 and from consistency across the whole effect vocabulary. Effects are declared in the element's unscaled units; `blur` radius 8 at `scale: 2` is sixteen frame pixels, and text `stroke_width` scales the same way. A mask that alone refused the transform would be the single exception in a seven-member closed list, and the exception would be silent at the schema level — nothing in `mask{shape, ...}` would tell a reader it lives in a different space than the `blur` sitting next to it in the same `effects` array. Worse, it would duplicate `clip`: a fixed, non-rotating, non-scaling frame-space porthole is *definitionally* what `clip` is, and ADR-0025 made it permanently static on purpose so that `scale` moves the picture behind a window that stays put — the Ken Burns. Post-transform mask would give the project two spellings of that porthole, which is the union the project has retired three times (ADR-0012, -0013, -0068). On the keyframe objection: "keyframes are transform-only" is a rule about *what may carry a keyframe list*, not a rule that nothing downstream of a transform may move. A `blur` on a scaling element already changes its frame-pixel width every frame, and nobody calls that a keyframed effect parameter. The mask member's own fields stay literal scalars, `validate` and `fmt` see no keyframe list inside an effect, and the invariant holds exactly as written. I'd have the ADR say this in one sentence so it is ratified rather than inferred.

**Trade-offs:** The real loss is the "fixed porthole the picture moves behind, but shaped" gesture — a circular Ken Burns. Under this vote that is unreachable in v1, since `clip` gives you the static window but only rectangular. That is a genuine gap and the ADR should name it as such rather than pretend it isn't one; the honest future answer is a shape option on `clip` or a soft/alpha mask, both already deferred. I also note this ratifies what the shipped renderer already does, which cuts both ways — it means no golden frames churn, but it also means the vote deserves extra scrutiny precisely because agreement with untested code is how ADR-0075 happened. My reason here is the `clip`-duplication argument, which stands independent of the code.

🗳️ **Juror 1** (Opus 5) — **VOTE Q4: (b) — a `review`-severity finding when a *param-less* `circle` sits on a non-square rect, naming the explicit-geometry repair. Specific to `circle`; `ellipse` and `rect` get nothing.**

**Reasoning:** The concern is specific and the packet identifies why: `circle` is the one shape whose param-less meaning *discards* part of its defining box. `rect` on a non-square rect is the rect; `ellipse` on a non-square rect is an ordinary oval that fills it — both are total, both are almost certainly what was meant. `circle` on a 1080x1912 element erases 832px, which is determinate but is a shape the author never typed a number for. That is the signature of a footgun rather than a decision. A `review` finding — not an error — is right because the behaviour is defined, the fixture legitimately relies on it, and ADR-0068's default must keep meaning what it means (the backward-compatibility constraint is absolute here). This vote depends on Q1 and is only honest because of it: the repair the finding names is "write the explicit centre and radius you meant, or use `ellipse` if you wanted it to fill the box." Under a Q1 vote of (b), no such repair exists and I would have been forced to (a), silence — which is a good illustration of why I voted (a) on Q1.

**Trade-offs:** Findings on determinate behaviour have a cost: they train authors to ignore `review` output, and a deliberate inscribed circle on a tall element now nags forever with no way to say "yes, I meant it" short of writing the explicit form. I consider that acceptable — writing the explicit form *is* the acknowledgement, and under ADR-0030 that spelling is itself content, so the file ends up recording the author's intent rather than leaning on arithmetic. An error (c) is clearly wrong: it would break the fixture's own migration path the moment anyone resizes a masked element, and it criminalises a meaning ADR-0068 just ratified. Option (d) — redefining `circle` on a non-square element — is the worst available: it either makes `circle` a second spelling of `ellipse` (a union) or invents a third behaviour nobody can predict from reading the file.
