🗳️ **Juror 2** (Opus 5.5) — **Part 1 VOTE: (c), but outside `blind_to`, as a fixed "reading floor" statement next to it. The self-check is acceptable only if it points the reader to the text as the authority, not as a pass/fail test.**

**Reasoning:** The effort's destination is that "I checked the whole thing" is an artifact rather than an argument. A Haiku-class reader that invents an umbrella scene and still reports coverage is the founding failure all over again, with the reader making the confident claim instead of the sampler. Montagent cannot choose the reader. What it can do is what it already does for the sampler: disclose, in fixed and learnable words, what the sheet assumes. The sheet does assume something about its reader, since the 140 px and 8 px floors are only real for a capable one. Leaving that assumption out of the answer is exactly the silence the effort exists to remove. It does not belong in `blind_to`. That block is defined as statements about the selection rule's structural blindness. Putting a reader-capability sentence there would blur a clean definition and invite the block to grow into a list of caveats. It should be a sibling fixed statement: constant, printed on every answer, judging nothing. The label/provenance redundancy is the right material to use, because it is the one thing Montagent knows exactly and the reader can compare against the pixels. But a reader that fails confidently will also grade itself confidently. Haiku transcribed the captions inside the frames and would likely "confirm" tile 1's label. So the sentence should not rest on self-grading. It should state the hierarchy: "The provenance list is the complete record of this range. The sheet is a picture of it, drawn for a reader who can read the 8 px gutter labels, of which tile 1's reads `<exact text>`. If you cannot find that text on the image, do not report visual findings from this sheet; use `frame --at <instant>`." That keeps the check, but its main job is to make the text outrank the picture. The text is the one channel every reader can read.

**Trade-offs:** It costs one more fixed line per answer, plus an ADR to create a new category of disclosure: about the reader, not the rule. That is a real change to the answer's contract. It is also unproven that weak readers act on it. (a) repeats the founding failure: a caller choosing a model does not know that model cannot see the sheet, and the tool does. (b) records the fact only where the reader never looks. The prior incident showed that what the reader sees in the answer and the tool description is what decides its behaviour, and ADR notes are neither.

🗳️ **Juror 2** (Opus 5.5) — **Part 2 VOTE: No**

**Reasoning:** The floors should be stated as what they are in pixels and reading tasks: "labels are 8 px; tiles are ≥140 px wide; the sheet assumes a reader who can read them." Which models met that bar is evidence, and evidence belongs in the ADRs, dated. The host-tier binding worked because it pointed at a documented, stable host property. "Sonnet/Opus-class" is neither documented nor stable. Model names change, classes blur, and a weak reader cannot know which class it belongs to, so a model name is no better as a self-check than the pixel fact. Naming a model in the tool description is worse: the description is where promises outrank facts, and a model name there would become a promise that goes stale while still sounding authoritative.

**Trade-offs:** A reader loses a quick "is this me?" shortcut, and must rely on the functional check (can you read tile 1's label?), which is less direct. "Only in the tool description" was rejected because it puts the claim most likely to go stale in the place that most outranks the answer's facts.

🗳️ **Juror 2** (Opus 5.5) — **Part 3 VOTE: (iii) decide now, and make the measurement a condition of shipping**

**Reasoning:** The argument settles Part 1. The sheet assumes something about its reader, and an assumption the effort relies on must be disclosed. No measurement could make that silence acceptable, so there is nothing to wait for. What the argument cannot settle is whether the chosen wording actually works: whether a weak reader given the sheet, the provenance list, `blind_to` and the reading-floor line then falls back to the text or to `frame --at`, or at least stops inventing. Shipping it untested would add another unverified promise to the answer, which is the pattern the prior incident warns about. The mocked plain-text prototype is cheap and answers exactly that question. If it shows no effect, the wording changes; the decision to disclose does not.

**Trade-offs:** Shipping of range mode now depends on a prototype ticket, and the result rests on very few readings per model. (i) risks shipping a sentence that looks like a defence and does nothing, which is quiet false assurance. (ii) holds the principle hostage to a measurement that cannot overturn it, only tune it.
